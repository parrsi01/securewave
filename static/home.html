FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about SecureWave VPN.

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet. It prevents your ISP, hackers, and third parties from monitoring your online activity. With public Wi-Fi attacks on the rise and ISPs selling browsing data, a VPN is essential for maintaining your digital privacy and security.
SecureWave uses the WireGuard protocol, which is significantly faster than older protocols like OpenVPN or IPSec. Most users retain over 90% of their base internet speed. In some cases, a VPN can actually improve speeds by bypassing ISP throttling on streaming and gaming traffic.
The number of simultaneous connections depends on your plan. The Starter plan supports 1 device, Pro supports up to 5, and Enterprise offers unlimited connections. You can protect your laptop, phone, tablet, and home devices across all supported platforms.
SecureWave focuses on legal, standards-based reliability: WireGuard keepalive, MTU tuning, and resilient reconnect behavior. Some networks may still block VPN protocols entirely, but these best-effort options can improve connectivity without impersonating other services or using domain fronting.
SecureWave provides a best-effort kill switch and reconnect behavior. On supported platforms, traffic is blocked if the tunnel drops unexpectedly to help prevent leaks, and the app will attempt to reconnect on network changes. For maximum protection on mobile, enable Always-on VPN and “block without VPN” in your OS settings.
